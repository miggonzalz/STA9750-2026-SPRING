0.1 Introduction

In this mini project, I analyze microdata from the American Time use Survey (ATUS) to explore how specific subgroups of Americans spend their daily time. With the help of R, I used dplyr to clean, merge, and summarize data from multiple data sources, then used ggplot2 to visualize key trends seen across demographics.

For the final deliverable, I am acting as product manager for a start-up launching a time-tracking app. Unlike typical calendar apps, this will compare users against demographically similar peers (e.g,. “Congrats! You exercised 3 more hours this week than others like you!”) Using gamification to encourage healthier time-use habits.

# --- FUNCTION: Load Activity Metadata ---
load_atus_activities <- function(){
    dest_file <- file.path("data", "mp02", "atus_activity_codes.csv")
    if(!file.exists(dest_file)){
        download.file("https://michael-weylandt.com/STA9750/mini/atus_activity_codes.csv", 
                      quiet=TRUE, destfile=dest_file, mode="wb")
    }
    # Standardize codes to 6-digit character strings for joining
    read_csv(dest_file, show_col_types = FALSE) |>
        mutate(across(starts_with("code"), ~str_pad(.x, 6, pad="0", side="left")))
}

After analyzing how thousands of people actually spend their days, we’ve identified exactly where our app ‘Be a Better You’,can step in and change people’s lives. We aren’t just building another time tracking app, we are building a roadmap to a more balanced life. Comparing “Typical” daily habits to our “Inspirational” benchmark (top 15% of their peers), we;ve found three ‘growth targets’ where our users are struggling the most. As a reminder, our goal isn’t to make them work harder, it is to help them live better.

Target Demographic Definitions Below are some top line points that will help you understand our three Growth segments:

TipCollege Undergrads: The ‘Sleep Hygeine’ Gap

Definition: Students aged 18–24 currently enrolled in a degree program.

These students are highly motivated but the data shows a gap when it comes to sleeping.Most are struggling to find a full night’s sleep more than likely because they are balancing the college experience with a heavy focus on their studies. Our app will help them redefine their life to increase and prioritize sleep. This will ensure that they find that time away from the books and release some stress by increasing their sleep time.

TipStay-at-Home Dads: The ‘Deep Connection’

Definition: These are are males with at least one child in the household who are not currently in the labor force.

After reviewing the data for this segment, I found a gap a gap in their child’s life that we can help bridge. Dads are home all day, their “Direct Child Care” (active,focused parenting) averages less than an hour a day while our “inspirational/high achievers” spend on average about four hours. With the help of our app, these four hours will become the “Gold Standard” for building a strong connection with their children.

TipYoung Professionals: Breaking the ‘Exercise Wall’

Definition: Employed adults aged 22–29 with no children, focused on fitness and career.

This segment is more than likely at the beginning stages of their career path and as such they sacrifice physical activity. Our analysis shows that young professionals spend very few minutes exercising, while our “Inspirational” group are averaging about an hour and a half of movement. We want to support these young professionals in prioritizing their own health. By showing that small moments of movement put them ahead of the typical peer and fuels their career success.

market_plot_data <- resp |>
  left_join(rost |> filter(line_no == 1) |> select(participant_id, age, sex), by = "participant_id") |>
  mutate(target_group = case_when(
    sex == "M" & children_count > 0 & employment_status == "Not in Labor Force" ~ "Stay-at-Home Dads",
    age >= 18 & age <= 24 & college_student == TRUE ~ "College Undergrads",
    age >= 22 & age <= 29 & employment_status == "Employed" & children_count == 0 ~ "Young Professionals",
    TRUE ~ NA_character_
  )) |>
  filter(!is.na(target_group)) |>
  inner_join(act, by = "participant_id") |>
  mutate(activity_name = case_when(
    str_starts(level1_code, "01") ~ "Sleeping",
    str_starts(level2_code, "0301") ~ "Childcare",
    str_starts(level1_code, "06") ~ "Education",
    str_starts(level1_code, "13") ~ "Exercise",
    TRUE ~ "Other"
  )) |>
  filter(activity_name != "Other") |>
  filter(time_spent_min > 0) |> 
  group_by(target_group, activity_name) |>
  summarise(
    low = quantile(time_spent_min / 60, 0.25, na.rm = TRUE),      
    typical = quantile(time_spent_min / 60, 0.50, na.rm = TRUE),  
    high = quantile(time_spent_min / 60, 0.75, na.rm = TRUE),     
    target = quantile(time_spent_min / 60, 0.85, na.rm = TRUE),   
    .groups = "drop"
  )

library(tidyverse)

# 1. Data Prep
plot_data <- tribble(
    ~Activity,   ~Target, ~Actual,
    "Exercise",  1.8,     1.2,
    "Education", 3.5,     4.2, 
    "Sleep",     8.5,     6.0  
) %>%
    mutate(
        fraction = Actual / Target,
        # Split the progress into two parts: the first 100% and the "Extra"
        base_progress = pmin(fraction, 1),
        extra_progress = pmax(0, fraction - 1),
        ring_pos = case_when(
            Activity == "Exercise" ~ 1,
            Activity == "Education" ~ 2,
            Activity == "Sleep" ~ 3
        )
    )

# 2. Plot
ggplot(plot_data, aes(x = ring_pos, fill = Activity)) +
    # Background Tracks (Dark Gray)
    geom_col(aes(y = 1), fill = "#2c2c2e", width = 0.8) +
    
    # Base Progress (Up to 100%)
    geom_col(aes(y = base_progress), width = 0.8, show.legend = FALSE) +
    
    # Extra Progress (The "Overlap" for Education)
    # We use a slightly different alpha or color to make it 'pop'
    geom_col(aes(y = extra_progress), width = 0.8, show.legend = FALSE, alpha = 0.8) +

    coord_polar(theta = "y") +
    scale_x_continuous(limits = c(0, 4)) +
